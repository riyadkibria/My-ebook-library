Mutual funds bought letter stock in private transactions, then immediately revalued these shares at a higher public price (see Grahams definition on p. 579). That enabled these go-go funds to report unsustainably high returns in the mid-1960s. The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission cracked down on this abuse in 1969, and it is no longer a concern for fund investors. Stock-option warrants are explained in Chapter 16.
